1. What is Decentralization? ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Let's first understand the basic idea.

Centralized system

In a centralized system, there is a central authority/master node controlling the system.

              CENTRAL SERVER
             /      |       \
            /       |        \
          N1        N2        N3

For example, your PPT gives conventional online service providers such as Google, Amazon, eBay and Apple's App Store as examples of the centralized model.

The central authority generally controls:

  • Data
  • Processing
  • Decision-making
  • Access

2. Decentralized System

In a decentralized system, control is distributed among multiple nodes rather than depending on one master node.

        N1 -------- N2
        | \         |
        |  \        |
        |   \       |
        N3 -------- N4
             \
              N5

Your PPT defines decentralization as a network where nodes are not dependent on a single master node, with control distributed among many nodes.

Key idea:

Centralization = concentration of control
Decentralization = distribution of control

3. Distributed ≠ Decentralized

This is an important conceptual distinction.

A system can be distributed without being fully decentralized.

For example:

Distributed:
        Central Controller
        /      |       \
      N1       N2      N3

Data/computation may be distributed across multiple machines, but one central authority still controls the system.

Your PPT similarly distinguishes distributed systems, where data and computation are spread across multiple nodes, from decentralization, where control is distributed rather than dependent on a single master node.

Therefore:

Distribution is about where computation/data exist.
Decentralization is about where control/decision-making resides.

This is the main question.

Traditional system:

             BANK

Instead of relying on one central organization to maintain the transaction record, participating blockchain nodes collectively maintain and agree on the ledger state.

Your PPT describes public/permissionless blockchain participants as maintaining ledger copies on their nodes and using distributed consensus to determine the eventual ledger state.

5. Why Decentralization is Useful

Your PPT lists several benefits.

1. Transparency

Information can be visible/shared across participants according to the blockchain's design.

2. Efficiency

Removing unnecessary intermediaries can streamline processes.

3. Cost saving

Reducing intermediaries can reduce some transaction/service costs.

4. Development of trusted ecosystems

Participants can interact according to shared rules rather than relying entirely on one central authority.

5. Anonymity

Some decentralized systems can provide pseudonymous participation.

These benefits are explicitly listed in your PPT.

6. Challenges of Decentralization

  • Security requirements

  • Human error

And the broader blockchain material identifies concerns such as:

  • Scalability
  • Adoption

  • Privacy/confidentiality

So an excellent exam answer should show both benefits and challenges.

7. Methods of Decentralization ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Your PPT identifies two methods:

1. Disintermediation

2. Competition / Contest-driven decentralization

This is a very likely 5-mark question.

8. Method 1 — Disintermediation ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

First understand the word.

Intermediary

An intermediary is a middleman between two parties.

Example:

Alice → BANK → Bob

The bank is the intermediary.

With blockchain:

Alice → Blockchain → Bob

The intermediary can be removed.

Your PPT gives exactly this example: money can be sent directly to a friend using the blockchain address without requiring a bank as the intermediary.

Therefore:

Disintermediation means removing the intermediary from a transaction/process.

9. Example of Disintermediation

Traditional model

Suppose Alice wants to send money to Bob in another country.

Alice
 ↓
Bank A
 ↓
Bank B
 ↓
Bob

There are intermediaries.

Blockchain model

Alice
 ↓
Blockchain
 ↓
Bob

The parties can interact directly through the blockchain system.

10. Disintermediation Beyond Finance

The concept isn't restricted to money.

Your PPT mentions applications in:

  • Health
  • Law
  • Public sector

For example, in healthcare, a patient could have greater control over their identity/data and share it directly with entities they trust rather than depending entirely on a central hospital record system.

11. Method 2 — Competition / Contest-Driven Decentralization

Here, we don't necessarily eliminate every service provider.

Instead, multiple providers compete.

Imagine smart contracts need an external data provider.

Provider A ─┐
Provider B ─┤
Provider C ─┼──→ Smart Contract
Provider D ─┘

The system can choose based on:

  • Reputation
  • Previous score
  • Reviews
  • Quality of service

Your PPT describes this competition model.

12. Does Competition Give Complete Decentralization?

No.

This is a very important point.

Your PPT explicitly says this method:

does not achieve complete decentralization

but helps prevent one intermediary/service provider from monopolizing the service.

So:

Disintermediation
       ↓
Can remove intermediary

Competition
       ↓
Multiple intermediaries compete
       ↓
Reduces monopoly
       ↓
But isn't full decentralization

21. Full Ecosystem Decentralization ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Now we reach a very important concept.

A common mistake is thinking:

"If I put something on blockchain, everything is decentralized."

Not necessarily.

Your PPT makes this point:

To achieve complete decentralization, the environment around the blockchain must also be decentralized.

Think:

             BLOCKCHAIN
                 ↓
      ┌──────────┼──────────┐
      ↓          ↓          ↓
   Storage   Communication  Compute
      ↓          ↓          ↓
   Other centralized dependencies

If the blockchain itself is decentralized but all its supporting services are controlled by one centralized provider, the overall ecosystem isn't fully decentralized.

22. Storage Problem ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Can blockchain store everything?

No.

Your PPT explicitly explains that although data can be stored directly on blockchain, blockchains aren't designed for storing large amounts of data such as images or large blobs.

Therefore, decentralized applications need decentralized storage solutions.

23. Distributed Hash Tables — DHT

A Distributed Hash Table (DHT) is a decentralized mechanism for storing and locating data across a peer-to-peer network.

Your PPT notes that DHTs were used in earlier peer-to-peer file-sharing systems including:

  • BitTorrent
  • Napster
  • Kazaa
  • Gnutella

24. Problem With Traditional P2P File Sharing

The problem isn't necessarily just storing the file.

It's:

Will someone continue storing it?

Your PPT points out that systems such as BitTorrent don't inherently incentivize users to keep files indefinitely. If the nodes holding needed data leave, the data may become unavailable.

Therefore, two important requirements are:

High availability

Data should be available when required.

Link stability

The network connections should remain accessible.

25. IPFS ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

26. Filecoin

DO

A decentralized organization distributes organizational control but still relies on human input to execute business logic.

DAO

A decentralized autonomous organization is automated and contains programmed logic that enables autonomous operation.

Your PPT explicitly describes DOs as relying on human input, while DAOs are autonomous and automated.
